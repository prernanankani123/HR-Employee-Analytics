HR Employee Data Analysis¶

Project Objective¶

  • The objective of this project is to analyze employee data using Python to understand employee satisfaction status, there salary, workload, and employee turnover across different departments. It also aims to find useful insights that can help an organization improve employee satisfaction and make better HR decisions.

Skills¶

  1. Python
  2. Pandas
  3. Data Cleaning & Preprocessing
  4. Missing Value Treatment.
  5. Exploratory Data Analysis(EDA)
  6. Data Visulization (Matplotlib & Seaborn)
  7. Business Insight Generation

Dataset Description¶

  • Dataset Name: HRDataset
  • Records: 311
  • Features: 36

Key Attributes¶

  • Salary Details: Salary
  • Job Information: Position, Department
  • Performance Indicators: PerformanceScore
  • Employee Satisfaction: EmpSatisfaction, EngagementSurvey
  • Employee Turnover: EmploymentStatus, Termd
  • Workload Indicators: SpecialProjectsCount, DaysLateLast30, Absences
  • Recruitment Details: RecruitmentSource

Step 3: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)¶

In [11]:
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import seaborn as sns

Q1.What is the distribution of employee salaries?¶

In [12]:
plt.figure(figsize=(10,5))
plt.hist(df['Salary'],bins=20,alpha=0.5)
plt.title("Distribution of Employee Salary",fontsize=20)
plt.xlabel("Salary")
plt.ylabel("Frequency")
plt.show()

Analysis :-¶

  • Most employees have salaries between approximately ₹45,000 and ₹75,000.
  • The distribution is right-skewed because a few employees have very high salaries compared to most employees.

Conclusion.¶

  • The salary distribution shows that most employees earn salaries within the lower salary range, while only a few employees receive very high salaries.

Q2.How does employee position affect salary levels?¶

In [23]:
avg_salary = df.groupby('Position')['Salary'].mean()
plt.figure(figsize=(10,8))
plt.barh(avg_salary.index,avg_salary.values,alpha=0.5,color="blue")
plt.title("Average Salary by Position",fontsize=20)
plt.xlabel("Position")
plt.ylabel("Salary")
plt.show()

Analysis :-¶

  • Employee salary varies significantly depending on their position.

Conclusion.¶

  • Senior leadership and director-level roles generally receive higher salaries, while technical support and entry-level positions tend to receive lower salaries.

Q3.Do employees with higher engagement scores tend to earn more?¶

In [14]:
sns.scatterplot(
    data=df, 
    x='EngagementSurvey', 
    y='Salary'
)
plt.title("Engagement Score vs Salary")
plt.xlabel("Engagement Score")
plt.ylabel("Salary")
plt.show()

Analysis :-¶

  • Most employees have engagement scores between 3.5 and 5.0, with salaries mainly between ₹45,000 and ₹80,000.

Conclusion.¶

  • Higher engagement scores do not necessarily lead to higher salaries.

Conclusion.¶

  • Some departments contain salary outliers, indicating that a few employees earn considerably more than others in the same department.

Q6.Does salary vary according to the number of special projects handled by employees?¶

In [17]:
avg_salary = df.groupby('SpecialProjectsCount')['Salary'].mean()

plt.figure(figsize=(10, 5))
plt.bar(avg_salary.index, avg_salary.values)

plt.title("Salary vs Number of Special Projects")
plt.xlabel("Number of Special Projects")
plt.ylabel("Average Salary")
plt.show()

Analysis :-¶

  • Employees handling 0 special projects have an average salary of approximately ₹61,000.
  • Employees handling 1 special project earn an average salary of around ₹63,000.
  • Employees handling 2 special projects have a higher average salary of approximately ₹100,000.
  • For employees handling 3–8 special projects, average salaries generally range between ₹86,000 and ₹99,000.

Conclusion.¶

  • There is a general positive relationship between the number of special projects and average salary, but it is not consistent. Handling more special projects does not always mean earning a higher salary.

Q7.Is Salary correlated with employee performance score?¶

In [18]:
corr = df[['Salary', 'PerfScoreID']].corr()

sns.heatmap(corr, 
            annot=True, 
            cmap='coolwarm')
plt.title("Salary vs Performance Score")
plt.show()

Analysis :-¶

  • The correlation coefficient between Salary and Performance Score is 0.13.
  • This indicates a very weak positive correlation between salary and employee performance score.

Conclusion.¶

  • Employees with higher performance scores do not necessarily earn significantly higher salaries.

Conclusion.¶
